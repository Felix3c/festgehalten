Palermo keeps saying what it will do and when: trains on the new underground rail section to
Politeama in October, the Ponte Corleone side bridge, the renovated swimming pool in viale del
Fante and the new street lights on the Costa Sud by the end of the year, the first works on two
new tram lines in summer 2027. This book writes it down, waits, and checks. It quotes one speaker:
the City of Palermo (Comune di Palermo, with Mayor Roberto Lagalla and the members of his
executive, the Giunta), in the news items on its own website. A statement goes in if it has a
date; intentions without one stay out. Each bet asks only whether the date in the quote was met,
never whether anyone meant it.

Why Palermo: the keeper's books now cover very large cities and capitals only. Palermo, the
fifth-largest city in Italy, comes after books on Rome, Naples and other European capitals and
large cities.

Sources: the news items of www.comune.palermo.it (“Novità”, in Italian). The website's robots.txt
(read 23 October 2026) excludes only the admin area; this book did not use it. The keeper took the
list of items from the website's sitemap and read 2,093 items dated 20 April to 21 October 2026,
on 23 October 2026. The City publishes about eleven items a day; most are traffic orders, notices,
library news and statements of individual council members, which the City also publishes. In them
the keeper looked for sentences that name a future date together with a promise word (215
sentences in 169 items, then a second pass of 218 sentences on large projects), then read each
candidate in its item. Thirteen sentences were checked live and in the archive; eleven became
bets. The news items carry no author line; the sender is the City. The date a statement was said
is the date on the item's own page. Two items are notes of committees of the City Council that
report what the City's technicians or a member of the Giunta said there; the bets say so.

Left out on purpose: statements of council members, committees and district councils rather than
the City's executive (among them the river park “Valle del Fiume Oreto”, whose deadline was given
by a committee chair); works run by other bodies, even when the City reports them (the biomethane
plant at the Bellolampo landfill, built and run by the company Asja Energy; the electricity grid;
the port; the bid for Euro 2032); calls, deadlines and events with a closing day; orders that
simply run until a date; deadlines that had already passed on 23 October 2026; and programmes
without a checkable end. Two bets rest on works done with others: the new rail section is run by
RFI, the national rail network, under a concession the City approved, and the Ponte Corleone side
bridges are built by Anas, the national road company, with the City's public works department.
They are in because the City's own item, and for the bridge the Mayor himself, gives the date.

Reading the dates: “a ottobre” (in October) means by 31 October 2026; a step that “si concluderà
il 21 novembre” (will end on 21 November) by 21 November 2026; “entro la fine dell'anno” and
“entro la fine del 2026” (by the end of the year, by the end of 2026) by 31 December 2026; “tra
agosto e settembre 2027” (between August and September 2027) by 30 September 2027; “nel 2027” (in
2027) by 31 December 2027. Each bet says so. Plain sentences (“si concluderà”, “è stata
confermata”, will end, was confirmed) count as announced (`angekuendigt`, 1.00). A sentence with
“prevista”, “previsto”, “prevediamo”, “presumibilmente”, “potranno”, “l'obiettivo” (foreseen, we
expect, presumably, can, the aim) counts as said with a reservation (`voraussichtlich`, 0.80).
Where one sentence holds two statements, the bet names the part it rests on. Only the quoted
sentence decides.

Language: this edition is in English. Quotations stay in the original Italian, exactly as
published, including the typographic apostrophe (’) where the City used it. The question under
each bet is the keeper's own wording and is the only thing that is resolved. English glosses of
Italian words in the text are the keeper's; they were made with the help of a language model and
have not yet been checked by a native speaker.

The speaker in this book is the City of Palermo as an institution. Names of officials are used
only to name a source. A bet is resolved "No" only with a primary source (a news item or page of
the City, a decision of the Giunta or the City Council, a notice on the City's notice board, a
procurement notice, a dated photo) and a date; if in doubt, it is marked `strittig` (disputed).
Pages of the City may be updated in place, so an entry can also be resolved by a dated copy of the
page.

The "Computer" in this book is a language model (Claude, Anthropic) with access to public
sources, not special software. It stands in the same list as the City, under the same rules.

Corrections: write to hallo@belegbar.eu (Italian, English and German are fine). Complaints are
checked within 48 hours and noted on the bet concerned; entries are never changed, only
annotated.

The keeper takes no money from the institutions measured here, with one exception: a published
fixed price for entries an institution lodges itself (festgehalten format v1, §8.4). So far:
none.

Expiry: an open bet expires two years after its check date (format §1.1, §2.3). Expired bets do
