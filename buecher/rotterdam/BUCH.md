Rotterdam keeps saying what it will do and when: a winter shelter open every day, paid parking
outside the ring, the end of free public transport for all pensioners, 3,500 new homes in 2027,
a renovated Boijmans museum by the end of 2030. This book writes it down, waits, and checks. It
quotes one speaker: the Municipality of Rotterdam (Gemeente Rotterdam, with its College of Mayor
and Aldermen), on its own website. A statement goes in if it has a date; intentions without one
stay out. Each bet asks only whether the date in the quote was met, never whether anyone meant it.

Why Rotterdam: the keeper's books now cover very large cities and capitals only. Rotterdam, the
second largest city of the Netherlands and the largest port of Europe, comes after London, Paris,
Hamburg, Munich, Vienna, Rome, Dublin, Copenhagen, Helsinki, Oslo, Brussels and Lyon.

Sources: the news of www.rotterdam.nl (“Nieuws”), found through its RSS feed and its sitemap. The
site lists news items only from 26 June 2026; all 44 items up to 16 October 2026 were read on
19 October 2026. In them the keeper looked for sentences that name a future date together with a
promise word (31 candidates, plus 13 with a day or month but no year), then read each candidate
in its item. Nineteen became bets. The date a statement was said is the date printed on the news
item.

Left out on purpose: deadlines of calls, consultations and applications; events and exhibitions;
works of other bodies that the City only reports (a building contractor's start on site; an
initiative of residents that the City “helps”); sentences of the coalition agreement of the five
parties, where the College's own budget says the same; a second sentence on free public
transport for older residents that repeats rotterdam-2026-006 and -008; a second sentence on the
winter shelter that repeats rotterdam-2026-002; plans with a hoped-for result (“hoopt”).

Reading the dates: a fixed day counts to that day (and hour, where one is given); a month to its
last day; “eind oktober” (end of October) to 31 October; “najaar” (autumn) to 30 November; a year
alone (“in 2027”) to 31 December. “Tot 2030” (until 2030) is set by the keeper to 31 March 2030,
the month of the next ordinary municipal elections and the end of the current term; “begin 2031”
(early 2031) is read in favour of the City as the whole year 2031; “vanaf 2028 jaarlijks” (yearly
from 2028) is checked for 2028. Each bet says so. Where a year names works without saying whether
they start or end, the keeper reads the start and the bet says so. A quoted sentence with “naar
verwachting” or “verwachting” (expected), “mogelijk” (possibly), “volgens (voorgestelde) planning”
(according to the (proposed) planning) or “wil” (wants) counts as said with a reservation
(`voraussichtlich`, 0.80); a plain statement or schedule line counts as announced
(`angekuendigt`, 1.00). Only the quoted sentence decides, with one exception that the bet names
(rotterdam-2026-009).

Language: this edition is in English. Quotations stay in the original Dutch, exactly as
published. The question under each bet is the keeper's own wording and is the only thing that
is resolved. English glosses of Dutch words in the text are the keeper's.

The speaker in this book is the Gemeente Rotterdam as an institution. Names of officials are used
only to name a source. A bet is resolved "No" only with a primary source (a page or news item of
the City, a document of the City Council, a permit, figures of CBS, a dated photo) and a date; if
in doubt, it is marked `strittig` (disputed). Pages of the City are updated in place, so an entry
can also be resolved by a dated copy of the page.

Quotations remain (c) Gemeente Rotterdam. The disclaimer page of rotterdam.nl (“Proclaimer”, read
19 October 2026) says: “Alle data op Rotterdam.nl zijn volledig openbaar en mogen hergebruikt
worden.” (all data on Rotterdam.nl are fully public and may be reused), except texts and images
with rights of third parties; it names no open licence. It sets one condition, which this book
keeps: “Het mag niet lijken dat de gemeente Rotterdam het zonder meer eens is met die beelden en
teksten.” This book is not endorsed by the Gemeente Rotterdam; the City has not agreed with it
or with any question in it. Short quotations also rest on art. 15a Auteurswet (not checked by a
lawyer). Everything else CC0. The source pages are not copied into this repository. For each
source there is a link to a Wayback Machine copy, and the SHA-256 checksum of the keeper's local
copy. Quotes were checked against the text after removing inline markup and collapsing repeated
spaces.

The "Computer" in this book is a language model (Claude, Anthropic) with access to public
sources, not special software. It stands in the same list as the City, under the same rules.

Corrections: write to hallo@belegbar.eu (Dutch, English and German are fine). Complaints are
checked within 48 hours and noted on the bet concerned; entries are never changed, only
annotated.

The keeper takes no money from the institutions measured here, with one exception: a published
fixed price for entries an institution lodges itself (festgehalten format v1, §8.4). So far:
none.

Expiry: an open bet expires two years after its check date (format §1.1, §2.3). Expired bets do
not count towards the hit rate, but they stay in the record.
