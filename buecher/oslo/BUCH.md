The City of Oslo (Oslo kommune) keeps saying what will be built and when: cycle paths, a
school, a sun deck, a reopened stream, a road over the new metro works. This book writes it
down, waits, and checks. It only quotes what the City itself published on its project pages
(“Slik bygger vi Oslo”, How we build Oslo, on oslo.kommune.no). A statement goes in if it has
a date; intentions without one stay out. Each bet asks only whether the date in the quote was
met, never whether anyone meant it.

Why Oslo: the keeper's books now cover very large cities and capitals only. Oslo comes after
London, Paris, Hamburg, Munich, Vienna, Rome, Dublin, Copenhagen and Helsinki.

Sources: the project pages under https://www.oslo.kommune.no/slik-bygger-vi-oslo/. The overview
loads by script, so the keeper used the City's sitemap: of 266 project pages, 112 had changed
since July 2026. On 18 October 2026 the keeper read those 112 pages; 118 sentences named a
date together with a building verb, nineteen were checked word for word, fifteen became bets.
The date a statement was said is the last change of its page in the sitemap. Left out on
purpose: weekly site notices (“uke 43”), dates already past, the upgrade of Thorleif Kleves
torg (the page already says the square is finished), one page that calls its own schedule
tentative (Majorstuveien and Middelthuns gate, summer 2028), a coastal path whose start is
given only as “winter 2027” (Bestumkilen; which winter is unclear), and a street upgrade
whose start is tied to another project in the same sentence (Stenersgata and Strandgata).
Two pages under “Slik bygger vi Oslo” are closed to robots by the City's robots.txt; they were
not fetched.

Reading the dates: a month counts to its last day; “høsten” (autumn) to 30 November, “våren”
(spring) to 30 June, “sommeren” (summer) to 31 August, “i løpet av” a year (during the year)
to 31 December. A sentence with “planlagt”, “planlegges”, “forventet”, “ventet” or “skal”
(planned, expected, is to) counts as said with a reservation (`voraussichtlich`, 0.80); a
plain schedule line counts as announced (`angekuendigt`, 1.00).

Language: this edition is in English. Quotations stay in the original Norwegian (Bokmål),
exactly as published. The question under each bet is the keeper's own wording and is the
only thing that is resolved. English glosses of Norwegian words in the text are the keeper's.

Speakers in this book are Oslo kommune as an institution or its agencies (mostly
Bymiljøetaten, the agency for urban environment, and Oslobygg KF). Names of officials are not
used. A bet is resolved "No" only with a primary source (the project page, a news item of the
City, a case in Saksinnsyn, a decision of the City Council) and a date; if in doubt, it is
marked `strittig` (disputed). Project pages are updated in place, so an entry can also be
resolved by a dated copy of the page.

Quotations remain (c) Oslo kommune (short quotation for review and discussion, åndsverkloven
§ 29; oslo.kommune.no publishes no re-use terms the keeper could find; not checked by a
lawyer); everything else CC0. The source pages are not copied into this repository. For each
source there is a link to a Wayback Machine copy, and the SHA-256 checksum of the keeper's
local copy. Quotes were checked against the text after removing inline markup and collapsing
repeated spaces.

The "Computer" in this book is a language model (Claude, Anthropic) with access to public
sources, not special software. It stands in the same list as the City, under the same rules.

Corrections: write to hallo@belegbar.eu (Norwegian, English and German are fine). Complaints
are checked within 48 hours and noted on the bet concerned; entries are never changed, only
annotated.

The keeper takes no money from the institution measured here, with one exception: a
published fixed price for entries an institution lodges itself (festgehalten format v1, §8.4). So far:
none.

Expiry: an open bet expires two years after its check date (format §1.1, §2.3). Expired bets
do not count towards the hit rate, but they stay in the record.
