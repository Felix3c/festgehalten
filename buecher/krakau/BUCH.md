Kraków keeps saying when things will be done: a 365 zł annual ticket for students from 1 November
2026, the renovation of Al. Słowackiego by the end of October, 50 more benches at the stops by
November, the Jordanówka pavilion by the middle of December, the tender for the tram to Azory and a
distance-based fare by January 2027. This book writes it down, waits, and checks. It quotes one
speaker: the City of Kraków (Gmina Miejska Kraków, with its City Hall and its units: the City Roads
Authority ZDMK, the Public Transport Authority ZTP, the City Investment Authority ZIM, the City
Greenery Authority ZZM, the city transport company MPK and a city hospital), in the news items on
its own website. A statement goes in if it has a date; intentions without one stay out. Each bet
asks only whether the date in the quote was met, never whether anyone meant it.

Why Kraków: the keeper's books now cover very large cities and capitals only. Kraków, Poland's
second city, comes after books on European capitals and large cities, most recently Belgrade,
Reykjavík and Luxembourg.

Why now: in 2026 the Mayor of Kraków was removed by a referendum (Polish press, read as a search
excerpt on 20 October 2026). After a period under an acting Mayor, Łukasz Gibała won the early
election in the second round on 11 October 2026 with 62.12 per cent, according to the City's own
news item, and takes office on 20 October 2026, the day this book opens. Almost all the statements
here were made by the City's administration under the acting Mayor. The book therefore also shows
whether the City keeps its word through a change at the top. The speaker stays the City as an
institution, whoever leads it.

Sources: the news items (“Aktualności”) of the City's platform “Magiczny Kraków” at www.krakow.pl
(in Polish). The legal notice of the site names the Gmina Miejska Kraków – Urząd Miasta Krakowa as
owner; every item used here names the City Hall's media office (“Referat Mediów Miejskich”) as
publisher. The website's robots.txt blocks only one search robot and allows these pages. The news
list gave the 298 most recent items, almost all dated 2 September to 19 October 2026, and the keeper
read all of them on 20 October 2026. In them the keeper looked for sentences that name a future date
together with a promise word (95 sentences, plus a second pass by hand), then read each candidate in
its item. Twenty sentences from sixteen items were checked live and in the archive; all twenty
became bets. On 27 October 2026 the keeper read the 42 items published since, checked all twenty
quotes live again (all still there word for word) and added three bets (021 to 023); for these
there is no archive copy yet, only the keeper's local copy with its checksum.
On 31 October 2026 the keeper read nine of the 19 items published since and added two bets (024 and
025), again with a local copy and checksum only.

Dates, said openly: the City updates its news items in place, under the same address, and sometimes
rewrites them (an item first published on 28 September described the run-off of 11 October). Each
item shows a date of publication (“Data publikacji”) and a date of last update (“Data
aktualizacji”). This book uses the date of the last update as the day the statement was made, and
quotes the version fetched on 20 October 2026; both dates are noted on each bet. For every source
there is a Wayback Machine copy saved on 20 October 2026 whose checksum equals that of the keeper's
live copy, so a later change by the City can be shown.

Weaknesses, said openly: most of the bets are about road and tram works, because that is what the
City's news items promise most often with a date. Three bets (krakau-2026-009, -013 and -019) come
from one item, and two (-010 and -015) from another. Two bets (-004 and -017) are end dates of works
that the City announced itself, not political promises; they are in the book because the City named
the date and they can be checked from the street.

Left out on purpose: works of other bodies, even when the City reports them (the airport terminal,
the access road to the airport and ul. Glogera, which belong to the airport or the region; works
paid by developers; new trams to be delivered by a manufacturer); events, festivals, exhibitions,
sports dates and campaigns; public consultations and open days; and project durations given without
a promise.

Reading the dates: “do końca października” (by the end of October) as 31 October 2026; “do 20
listopada” (until 20 November) as finished by that day; “do listopada br.” (by November this year)
in favour of the City as by 30 November 2026; “na połowę grudnia” (for the middle of December) as by
15 December 2026; “w grudniu”, “do końca bieżącego roku” and “jeszcze w tym roku” (in December, by
the end of this year, still this year) as by 31 December 2026; “do końca stycznia 2027”, “od stycznia
2027” and “na przełomie 2026 i 2027 roku” (by the end of January, from January, at the turn of the
year) in favour of the City as by 31 January 2027; “do lipca 2027” (until July 2027) in favour of the
City as by 31 July 2027; “pod koniec października 2027” (at the end of October 2027) as by 31 October
2027; “na 2027 r.” (for 2027) as begun in 2027; “w 2029 r.” (in 2029) as by 31 December 2029. Each
bet says so. Plain future sentences (“zostanie”, “potrwają do”; will be, will last until) count as
announced (`angekuendigt`, 1.00). Sentences that only say what is planned or expected (“planowane
jest”, “zaplanowano”, “mają zakończyć się”, “ma być”, “przewidziane”, “powinni”; is planned, are to
be finished, should) count as said with a reservation (`voraussichtlich`, 0.80). Only the quoted
sentence decides.

Elections: the current term of the Mayor and the City Council runs until the next regular local
elections in Poland, due in 2029; the early election of 2026 did not start a new term (Polish
press, read as a search excerpt on 20 October 2026; the keeper did not read an official source and
the exact date is not known).

Language: this edition is in English. Quotations stay in the original Polish, exactly as published.
The question under each bet is the keeper's own wording and is the only thing that is resolved.
English glosses of Polish words in the text are the keeper's; they were made with the help of a
language model and have not yet been checked by a native speaker.

The speaker in this book is the City of Kraków as an institution. Names of officials are used only
to name a source. A bet is resolved "No" only with a primary source (a news item or page of the City
or its units, a timetable or tariff of ZTP or MPK, a notice on the Polish public procurement portals,
a resolution of the City Council, a dated photo) and a date; if in doubt, it is marked `strittig`
(disputed).

Corrections: write to hallo@belegbar.eu (Polish, English and German are fine). Complaints are
checked within 48 hours and noted on the bet concerned; entries are never changed, only
annotated.

The keeper takes no money from the institutions measured here, with one exception: a published

none.

Expiry: an open bet expires two years after its check date (format §1.1, §2.3). Expired bets do
not count towards the hit rate, but they stay in the record.
