The City of Luxembourg keeps saying what it will do and when: a third “E-Reider” terminal in the
Parc de Gasperich at the end of October, an orchard in Cessange in autumn, new retaining walls in
the Pétrusse valley by the end of January 2027, sun protection for 19 more school buildings by
summer 2027, and street works all over town with an expected end on each site page. This book
writes it down, waits, and checks. It quotes one speaker: the City of Luxembourg (Ville de
Luxembourg), on its own website, including what its mayor and aldermen answer to councillors in
the City Council. A statement goes in if it has a date; intentions without one stay out. Each bet
asks only whether the date in the quote was met, never whether anyone meant it.

Why Luxembourg: the keeper's books now cover very large cities and capitals only. Luxembourg, the
capital of the Grand Duchy, comes after books on other European capitals and large cities, most
recently Naples and Bratislava.

Sources: three parts of www.vdl.lu (in French), all read on 19 October 2026. (1) The news items
(“Actualités”): the list holds only 60 items, about 42 of them since 20 April 2026, so news alone
would not carry a book. (2) The pages of current construction sites (“Chantiers en cours”), which
give for each site a start and an expected end (“fin prévisible”). (3) The pages of the City Council
meetings of 2026 and the written answers to councillors' questions, where the mayor Lydie Polfer
and the aldermen answer word for word. The website's robots.txt (read 19 October 2026) excludes only
two media folders; this book used nothing there. In these pages the keeper looked for sentences
that name a future date together with a promise word (129 sentences from 74 pages and 46 from the
council pages), then read each candidate in its page. Seventeen sentences were checked live and in
the archive; all seventeen became bets.

Dates said: news items and council minutes carry their own date, and that date is used. The
construction-site pages, two press-file pages and the written answers of September 2026 show no
publication date. For them the bet uses the date of the oldest Wayback Machine copy that already
has the quoted words; where there is none, the day the keeper read the page (19 October 2026). Each
bet says which.

Left out on purpose: works of the state or of other bodies, even when the City reports them (a
filtering plant, the tram extension of Luxtram, police video surveillance); drinking water from a
new source “from autumn” (no measure to check it by); procedure deadlines of planning files; a
second orchard in Bonnevoie in autumn 2027 (in the same sentence as luxemburg-2026-003, not bet
separately); street works whose expected end had already passed on 19 October 2026 but which were
still listed as current (Clausen, rue Pierret, rue Charles Martel, rue du Pont). That last finding
is noted here and is not a bet.

Reading the dates: a month counts to its last day; “fin octobre” (end of October) to 31 October
2026; “automne” (autumn) is read as meteorological autumn, so to 30 November; “printemps”
(spring) to 31 May; “été” (summer) to 31 August; “début de l'année 2027” (beginning of 2027) to 31
March 2027; “d'ici 2030” (by 2030) to 31 December 2030; “la rentrée 2030” (the start of the 2030
school year) is set by the keeper to 15 September 2030. Each bet says so. A plain sentence or a
plain list line (“sera”, “seront”, “jusqu'en”, will be, until) counts as announced
(`angekuendigt`, 1.00). A sentence or schedule line with “prévisible”, “prévisionnelle” (expected),
“prévu(e)” (planned), “devraient” (should) or “selon toutes prévisions” (by all forecasts) counts as
said with a reservation (`voraussichtlich`, 0.80). Where one sentence holds several statements, the
bet names the part it rests on. Only the quoted sentence decides.

Language: this edition is in English. Quotations stay in the original French, exactly as
published. The question under each bet is the keeper's own wording and is the only thing that is
resolved. English glosses of French words in the text are the keeper's; they were made with the
help of a language model and have not been checked by a native speaker.

The speaker in this book is the City of Luxembourg as an institution. Names of officials are used
only to name a source. A bet is resolved "No" only with a primary source (a page or news item of
the City, minutes of the City Council, a procurement notice, a dated photo) and a date; if in
doubt, it is marked `strittig` (disputed). Pages of the City are updated in place, so an entry can
also be resolved by a dated copy of the page.

Licence of the quotes: the City's legal notice for its digital media (“Dispositions légales
relatives à nos supports digitaux”, read 19 October 2026) says that the texts of vdl.lu are under
Creative Commons BY-SA 3.0 Luxembourg and may be reused with the source “vdl.lu - Site Internet de
la Ville de Luxembourg” and a direct link, under the same licence; photos and videos are excluded.
The same notice also keeps an older paragraph allowing use “strictly personal” only, so the page
contradicts itself. This book follows the open licence: each bet names the source with a link and
says the quote stays under CC BY-SA 3.0 LU. Whether ShareAlike reaches beyond the quote to the
keeper's own text of a bet is unclear (not checked by a lawyer); the keeper's own text is meant as
CC0. This book uses no pictures or coats of arms. It is not endorsed by the City of Luxembourg;
the City has not agreed with it or with any question in it. The source pages are not copied into
this repository. For each source there is a link to a Wayback Machine copy and the SHA-256 checksum
of the keeper's local copy. Quotes were checked against the text of the page after removing
markup, decoding entities and collapsing repeated spaces.

The "Computer" in this book is a language model (Claude, Anthropic) with access to public
sources, not special software. It stands in the same list as the City, under the same rules.

Corrections: write to hallo@belegbar.eu (French, English and German are fine). Complaints are
checked within 48 hours and noted on the bet concerned; entries are never changed, only
annotated.

The keeper takes no money from the institutions measured here, with one exception: a published
fixed price for entries an institution lodges itself (festgehalten format v1, §8.4). So far:
none.

Expiry: an open bet expires two years after its check date (format §1.1, §2.3). Expired bets do
not count towards the hit rate, but they stay in the record.
