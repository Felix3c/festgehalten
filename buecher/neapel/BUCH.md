Naples keeps saying what it will do and when: a new metro station at Tribunali before Christmas,
the seafront tram running and the Villa Comunale finished by the end of the year, the Bagnoli pier
open again after about five months, a new land-use plan in 2027, the museum under the Duomo station
in 2028. This book writes it down, waits, and checks. It quotes one speaker: the City of Naples
(Comune di Napoli, with Mayor Gaetano Manfredi and the members of his executive, the Giunta), in
the news items on its own website. A statement goes in if it has a date; intentions without one
stay out. Each bet asks only whether the date in the quote was met, never whether anyone meant it.

Why Naples: the keeper's books now cover very large cities and capitals only. Naples, the
third-largest city in Italy, comes after books on Rome and on other European capitals and large
cities, most recently Zagreb, Sofia and Ljubljana.

Sources: the news items of www.comune.napoli.it (“Novità”, in Italian). The website's robots.txt
(read 19 October 2026) excludes only the admin area and the search; this book used neither. The
keeper took the list of items from the website's sitemap and read 1,358 items dated 20 April to
19 October 2026, on 19 October 2026; 543 routine notices (traffic orders, weather and fire
warnings, sales bans, service conferences) were left out by their address. The City publishes
about seven items a day, most of them on events, calls and council committees. In them the keeper
looked for sentences that name a future date together with a promise word (237 sentences in 152
items, then two more passes), then read each candidate in its item. Sixteen sentences were checked
live and in the archive; twelve became bets. The news items carry no author line; the sender is the
City. The date a statement was said is the publication date on the item's own page. Two items come
from the press office of the City Council, which reports what members of the Giunta said in the
Council or its committees; the bets say so.

Left out on purpose: works of other bodies or shared with them, even when the City reports them
(Metro Line 10 to Afragola, contracted by the regional company EAV; the airport, the port, the
Region of Campania, the police headquarters' cameras); statements of council members rather than
the City's executive; targets of the waste company ASIA; events, calls and deadlines with a closing
day; orders that simply run until a date; deadlines that had already passed on 19 October 2026;
programmes without a checkable end; a forecourt at the Agnano racecourse to be handed to its
operator by 1 December 2026, and video surveillance in Ponticelli by the end of the year, because
both are hard to check from outside; and the delivery of a design for the former bakery “Ex Forno”,
which is a planning step only. The City itself says that the national deadline of December 2026
for the housing works at the “bipiani” in Ponticelli will hardly be met; that admission is not a
bet.

Reading the dates: “alla fine di novembre” (at the end of November) means by 30 November 2026;
“in autunno” (in autumn) is read as meteorological autumn, by 30 November; “prima di Natale”
(before Christmas) as by 24 December 2026; “entro l'anno”, “entro (la) fine (dell')anno”, “entro
fine 2026”, “entro il 2026” and “entro dicembre 2026” (within the year, by the end of the year,
by 2026, by December 2026) as by 31 December 2026; a closure “per circa 5 mesi” (for about five
months) from 14 September in favour of the City as by 28 February 2027; “nel corso del 2027” and
“nel 2027” (during 2027, in 2027) as by 31 December 2027; “nel 2028” as by 31 December 2028. Each
bet says so. Plain sentences (“sarà”, “verranno”, “avverrà”, will be, will happen) count as
announced (`angekuendigt`, 1.00). A sentence with “prevista”, “si stima”, “dovrebbe” (foreseen,
it is estimated, should), or a plain sentence tied to “compatibilmente con le risorse disponibili”
(as far as funds allow), counts as said with a reservation (`voraussichtlich`, 0.80). Where one
sentence holds two statements, the bet names the part it rests on. Only the quoted sentence
decides.

Language: this edition is in English. Quotations stay in the original Italian, exactly as
published, including the typographic apostrophe (’) where the City used it. The question under
each bet is the keeper's own wording and is the only thing that is resolved. English glosses of
Italian words in the text are the keeper's; they were made with the help of a language model and
have not yet been checked by a native speaker.

The speaker in this book is the City of Naples as an institution. Names of officials are used
only to name a source. A bet is resolved "No" only with a primary source (a news item or page of
the City, a decision of the Giunta or the City Council, a notice on the City's notice board, a
procurement notice, a dated photo) and a date; if in doubt, it is marked `strittig` (disputed).
Pages of the City may be updated in place, so an entry can also be resolved by a dated copy of the
page.

The "Computer" in this book is a language model (Claude, Anthropic) with access to public
sources, not special software. It stands in the same list as the City, under the same rules.

Corrections: write to hallo@belegbar.eu (Italian, English and German are fine). Complaints are
checked within 48 hours and noted on the bet concerned; entries are never changed, only
annotated.

The keeper takes no money from the institutions measured here, with one exception: a published
fixed price for entries an institution lodges itself (festgehalten format v1, §8.4). So far:
none.

Expiry: an open bet expires two years after its check date (format §1.1, §2.3). Expired bets do
not count towards the hit rate, but they stay in the record.
