Edinburgh keeps saying what it will do and when: Firework Control Zones from 30 October to
8 November 2026, the first visitor levy receipts in October and November 2026, a revised Princes
Street and Waverley Strategy in early 2027, a redesigned cultural funding programme agreed in early
2027 with money from April 2028, close to £50 million a year from the levy by 2028/29, a million
trees by 2030. This book writes it down, waits, and checks. It quotes one speaker: the City of
Edinburgh Council (with its committees and the Council Leader), in the news releases on its own
website. A statement goes in if it has a date; intentions without one stay out. Each bet asks only
whether the date in the quote was met, never whether anyone meant it.

Why Edinburgh: the keeper's books now cover very large cities and capitals only. Edinburgh, the
capital of Scotland, comes after books on other European capitals and large cities, most recently
Rotterdam, Riga and Bucharest.

Sources: the news releases of www.edinburgh.gov.uk (“News”). The keeper read the 116 news releases
dated 20 April to 12 October 2026 on 19 October 2026. In them the keeper looked for sentences that
name a future date together with a promise word (46 sentences), then read each candidate in its
release. Fourteen sentences from 14 releases became 15 bets; one sentence gives two bets
(edinburgh-2026-007 and -015). Each bet links the page of the release. The releases carry no
author line; the sender is the Council. The date a statement was said is the “Published” date of
the release. One source is the Council Leader's monthly report, published in the same news section
(edinburgh-2026-012).

Update 25 October 2026: the keeper read the seven releases of 19 to 22 October 2026. Two sentences
from one release became edinburgh-2026-016 and -017 (Silverlea homes and green space); a third
repeats the date of edinburgh-2026-002 and is noted there.

Left out on purpose: works of other bodies, even when the Council reports them (the Scottish
Government and Transport Scotland, Network Rail, the regional transport partnership SEStran,
developers, Edinburgh Leisure, event organisers), and joint programmes paid for by the Scottish
Government; deadlines that had already passed on 19 October 2026; exhibitions and services that
only name a closing day; plans without a checkable result.

Reading the dates: a fixed day counts to that day; a month to its last day; “later this year” and
a year alone to 31 December; “early 2027” to the end of the first quarter, 31 March 2027; a
financial year (“2026/27”) to its last day, 31 March; “from April 2027” (the earliest month) is
read in favour of the Council as by 30 April 2027. Each bet says so. Plain sentences of the
Council (“will”, “will be agreed”, “includes a commitment”) count as announced (`angekuendigt`,
1.00). A quoted sentence with “expected”, “we expect”, “could” or “ambitious target” counts as said
with a reservation (`voraussichtlich`, 0.80). Only the quoted sentence decides.

Term of office: the next Scottish local government election is on Thursday 6 May 2027. Bets
edinburgh-2026-012 to -015 fall due after it; they stay with the Council as an institution, not
with any administration.

Language: this edition is in English; quotations are given exactly as published. The question
under each bet is the keeper's own wording and is the only thing that is resolved.

The speaker in this book is the City of Edinburgh Council as an institution. Names of officials
are used only to name a source. A bet is resolved "No" only with a primary source (a news release
or page of the Council, committee papers, a procurement notice, a dated photo) and a date; if in
doubt, it is marked `strittig` (disputed). Pages of the Council may be updated in place, so an
entry can also be resolved by a dated copy of the page.

Quotations remain (c) The City of Edinburgh Council. The Council's “Terms and disclaimer” page
(read 19 October 2026) says that all material on the website “is for personal, non-commercial use”
and that no part “may be copied or adapted without the written permission of the City of Edinburgh
Council”; the keeper found no open licence for the texts. This book is not endorsed by the City of
Edinburgh Council; the Council has not agreed with it or with any question in it. Each bet quotes
one sentence, with the title of the release, its date and a link. This rests on the Copyright,
Designs and Patents Act 1988, s. 30(1ZA) (quotation, for criticism, review or otherwise, of a work
made available to the public, as fair dealing, no more than the purpose requires, with
acknowledgement) and s. 30(4) (a contract term that would prevent such a quotation is
unenforceable) (not checked by a lawyer). Everything else CC0. The source pages are not copied into
this repository. For each source there is a link to a Wayback Machine copy and the SHA-256 checksum
of the keeper's local copy. Quotes were checked against the text of the release after removing
inline markup, decoding entities, writing typographic apostrophes as plain ones and collapsing
repeated spaces.

The "Computer" in this book is a language model (Claude, Anthropic) with access to public
sources, not special software. It stands in the same list as the Council, under the same rules.

Corrections: write to hallo@belegbar.eu (English and German are fine). Complaints are checked
within 48 hours and noted on the bet concerned; entries are never changed, only annotated.

The keeper takes no money from the institutions measured here, with one exception: a published
fixed price for entries an institution lodges itself (festgehalten format v1, §8.4). So far:
none.

Expiry: an open bet expires two years after its check date (format §1.1, §2.3). Expired bets do
not count towards the hit rate, but they stay in the record.
