Riga keeps saying what it will do and when: a higher tourist tax from 1 January 2027, the
Zemitāns bridge back in service by the end of 2026, shelters in 80 more schools, passenger boats
on the Daugava next spring, the Vanšu bridge rebuilt by 2029. This book writes it down, waits,
and checks. It quotes one speaker: the City of Riga (Rīgas valstspilsētas pašvaldība, with its
City Council, Rīgas dome, its committees and departments), on its own website. A statement goes in
if it has a date; intentions without one stay out. Each bet asks only whether the date in the
quote was met, never whether anyone meant it.

Why Riga: the keeper's books now cover very large cities and capitals only. Riga, the capital of
Latvia and the largest city of the Baltic states, comes after London, Paris, Hamburg, Munich,
Vienna, Rome, Dublin, Copenhagen, Helsinki, Oslo, Brussels, Lyon and Rotterdam.

Sources: the news of www.riga.lv (“Jaunumi”, in Latvian; the City publishes no English news
section), found through its sitemap. The 932 news items published from 19 April to 16 October
2026 were read on 19 October 2026. In them the keeper looked for sentences that name a future
date together with a promise word (173 sentences), then read each candidate in its item. Twenty
sentences became 22 bets; two sentences name two deadlines and give two bets each
(riga-2026-016 and -021, riga-2026-017 and -022). All quoted items carry the author line
“Autors: Rīgas pašvaldības Komunikācijas pārvaldes Ārējās komunikācijas nodaļa” (the City's
external communication unit). The date a statement was said is the date printed on the item
(“Publicēts”).

Left out on purpose: items written by other bodies, even when the City owns them (the Freeport of
Riga, Rīgas satiksme, Rīgas ūdens, the Riga Investment and Tourism Agency), and joint plans with
the State; deadlines of calls, consultations and applications; events; traffic orders and road
closures; planning documents whose result cannot be checked; a second sentence that repeats a
bet already in the book (Esplanāde and Latgale park “nākamgad”, the Ziepniekkalns overpass of
the same project as riga-2026-007).

Reading the dates: a fixed day counts to that day; a month to its last day (“līdz 2027. gada
maijam”, by May 2027, is read in favour of the City as by 31 May); a quarter to its last day;
“rudenī” (in autumn) to 30 November; “pavasarī” (in spring) to 31 May; a year alone, “līdz …
gada beigām” (by the end of …) and “… gada otrajā pusē” (in the second half of …) to 31 December;
“līdz 2030. gadam” (until 2030) is read in favour of the City as the end of 2030; “mācību gada
sākums” (start of the school year) as 1 September. Each bet says so. Where a year names works
without saying whether they start or end, the bet says which is meant. Planning words are the
normal style of the City's news: a quoted sentence with “plānots”, “plānota” or “plāno”
(planned, plans), “paredzēts” (foreseen) or “varētu” (could) counts as said with a reservation
(`voraussichtlich`, 0.80); a plain statement or a Council decision counts as announced
(`angekuendigt`, 1.00). Only the quoted sentence decides.

Language: this edition is in English. Quotations stay in the original Latvian, exactly as
published. The question under each bet is the keeper's own wording and is the only thing that is
resolved. English glosses of Latvian words in the text are the keeper's; they were made with the
help of a language model and have not yet been checked by a native speaker.

The speaker in this book is the City of Riga as an institution. Names of officials are used only
to name a source. A bet is resolved "No" only with a primary source (a page or news item of the
City, a decision of the City Council, a procurement notice, a permit, a dated photo) and a date;
if in doubt, it is marked `strittig` (disputed). Pages of the City are updated in place, so an
entry can also be resolved by a dated copy of the page.

Quotations remain (c) Rīgas valstspilsētas pašvaldība. The footer of riga.lv (read 19 October
2026) says: “© 2026 Rīgas valstspilsētas pašvaldība, publicētā satura visas tiesības
aizsargātas.” (all rights in the published content reserved); the keeper found no open licence
for the texts. This book is not endorsed by the Rīgas valstspilsētas pašvaldība; the City has not
agreed with it or with any question in it. Each bet quotes one sentence, with the title of the
item, its author line, its date and a link. This rests on the Latvian Copyright Law
(Autortiesību likums): art. 6 leaves documents issued by state and municipal institutions and
news of the day outside copyright, and art. 20 allows quotation for critical purposes to the
extent the purpose needs, naming the source and the author (not checked by a lawyer). Everything
else CC0. The source pages are not copied into this repository. For each source there is a link
to a Wayback Machine copy, and the SHA-256 checksum of the keeper's local copy. Quotes were
checked against the text after removing inline markup and collapsing repeated spaces.

The "Computer" in this book is a language model (Claude, Anthropic) with access to public
sources, not special software. It stands in the same list as the City, under the same rules.

Corrections: write to hallo@belegbar.eu (Latvian, English and German are fine). Complaints are
checked within 48 hours and noted on the bet concerned; entries are never changed, only
annotated.

The keeper takes no money from the institutions measured here, with one exception: a published
fixed price for entries an institution lodges itself (festgehalten format v1, §8.4). So far:
none.

Expiry: an open bet expires two years after its check date (format §1.1, §2.3). Expired bets do
not count towards the hit rate, but they stay in the record.
