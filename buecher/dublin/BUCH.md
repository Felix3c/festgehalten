Dublin City Council keeps saying what will happen and when: new homes, parks, sports
grounds, a market hall, cycle tracks. This book writes it down, waits, and checks. It only
quotes what the Council itself published: its news releases on dublincity.ie and its own
reports to the City Council. A statement goes in if it has a date or a number; intentions
without either stay out. Each bet asks only whether the date or number in the quote was met,
never whether anyone meant it.

Why Dublin: the keeper's books now cover very large cities and capitals only. Dublin comes
after London, Paris, Hamburg, Munich, Vienna and Rome.

Sources: the news list at https://www.dublincity.ie/news (136 releases from 19 April to 15
October 2026, read on 18 October 2026) and the Chief Executive's Report No. 78/2026 “Capital
Programme 2026 - 2028” (signed 2 April 2026, 96 pages, PDF on dublincity.ie). 29 statements
with a future date were checked word for word; 15 became bets. Left out on purpose: events,
plans without a date, targets for 2028 and later, promises made mainly by others (the Land
Development Agency, housing bodies, the Office of Public Works), and one promise whose date
had already passed when this book was opened (Merrion Square Tea Rooms, Q3 2026).

The Capital Programme gives two different dates for the same thing twice: the start of
construction at Dalymount Park (end of 2026 on one page, early 2027 on another) and the
completion of the Fruit and Vegetable Market (Q3 2027 and late 2027). Both are noted on the
bets concerned.

Language: this edition is in English, and so are the quotations, exactly as published. The
question under each bet is the keeper's own wording and is the only thing that is resolved.

Speakers in this book are Dublin City Council as an institution or its Chief Executive in
office. Names of officials are not used. A bet is resolved "No" only with a primary source
(news of the Council, a report to the City Council, a published list) and a date; if in doubt,
it is marked `strittig` (disputed).

Quotations are public sector information of Dublin City Council, re-used under the terms of
its website (PSI licence; CC-BY is the standard PSI licence in Ireland since circular 12/2016;
not checked by a lawyer). Source: Dublin City Council. Everything else CC0. The source pages
are not copied into this repository. For each source there is a link to a Wayback Machine
copy, and the SHA-256 checksum of the keeper's local copy. Quotes were checked against the
text after removing inline markup and collapsing repeated spaces.

The "Computer" in this book is a language model (Claude, Anthropic) with access to public
sources, not special software. It stands in the same list as the Council, under the same
rules.

Corrections: write to hallo@belegbar.eu (English and German are fine). Complaints are checked
within 48 hours and noted on the bet concerned; entries are never changed, only annotated.

The keeper takes no money from the institution measured here, with one exception: a
published fixed price for entries an institution lodges itself (festgehalten format v1, §8.4). So far:
none.

Expiry: an open bet expires two years after its check date (format §1.1, §2.3). Expired bets
do not count towards the hit rate, but they stay in the record.
