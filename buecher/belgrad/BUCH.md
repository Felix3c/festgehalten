Belgrade keeps saying when its road works will be done: a street in Zemun open again by 3 November
2026, a corridor to the clinical centre at Bežanijska kosa finished by the end of November, the
link to the Most na Adi bridge by the new year, a new street under the railway open by the end of
January 2027, and Bulevar patrijarha Pavla at full capacity when EXPO 2027 opens on 15 May 2027.
This book writes it down, waits, and checks. It quotes one speaker: the City of Belgrade (Grad
Beograd, with Mayor Aleksandar Šapić and the City's Secretariat for Public Transport), in the news
items on its own website. A statement goes in if it has a date; intentions without one stay out.
Each bet asks only whether the date in the quote was met, never whether anyone meant it.

Why Belgrade: the keeper's books now cover very large cities and capitals only. Belgrade, the
capital of Serbia, comes after books on other European capitals and large cities, most recently
Ljubljana, Zagreb and Reykjavík.

Sources: the news items of www.beograd.rs (“Vesti”, in Serbian, Latin script, under `/lat/`). All
items used here are from Beoinfo, which the City's own website describes as the news service of
the City's Secretariat for Information; its legal form was not checked. The website's robots.txt
allows these pages. The City publishes about three and a half news items a day, most of them on
bus and tram detours, water cuts and events, so the keeper read the 300 most recent, dated 28 July
to 19 October 2026, on 20 October 2026. In them the keeper looked for sentences that name a future
date together with a promise word (37 sentences, plus a second pass by hand), then read each
candidate in its item. Fourteen sentences were checked live and in the archive; all fourteen
became bets. The date a statement was said is the date on the item's own page.

One weakness, said openly: eight of the fourteen bets come from one item, the Mayor's site visit
of 16 October 2026, and two more from his item of 18 October 2026 on new tenders. The City's
dated promises are rare and come in bundles. Four bets (belgrad-2026-001, -004, -005 and -010)
are end dates of road closures that the City announced itself, not political promises; they are
in the book because the City named the date and can be checked from the street.

Left out on purpose: works of other bodies, even when the City reports them (the new clinic for
gynaecology and obstetrics, announced by the President of Serbia and paid by the state; the state
road at Mladenovac; houses bought for refugees by a district and a state commissariat); agency
texts the City only passes on (“prenosi Tanjug”); events, exhibitions and sports dates (the book
fair, EXPO 2027 as a date of its own, matches in 2027 and 2029); deadlines that are too vague (a
kindergarten “in the next days”); and results of competitions.

Reading the dates: a closure “od … do 3. novembra” (from … to 3 November) means open again by that
day; “za mesec dana” (in a month) counts from the day it was said; “do kraja novembra” and “do
kraja januara” (by the end of November, of January) as the last day of that month; “do nove
godine” and “do kraja godine” (by the new year, by the end of the year) as 31 December 2026;
“početkom 2027. godine” (at the beginning of 2027) as by 31 March 2027; “do proleća 2027” (by
spring 2027) in favour of the City as by 31 May 2027; “do tada” (by then) as the opening of EXPO
on 15 May 2027, named in the sentence before; “u 2027. godini” (in 2027) as the calendar year
2027. Each bet says so. Plain sentences (“će”, will; “ćemo”, we will) count as announced
(`angekuendigt`, 1.00). A quoted sentence with “očekujemo” or “očekuje se” (we expect, it is
expected) counts as said with a reservation (`voraussichtlich`, 0.80). Only the quoted sentence
decides.

Elections: the last election to the City Assembly of Belgrade was on 2 June 2024; the next regular
one is due in 2028 (term of four years; English Wikipedia, “City Assembly of Belgrade”, read 20
October 2026; the keeper did not read an official source for 2028). Whether there will be an early
election is not known. The speaker in this book stays the City as an institution, whoever governs
it.

Language: this edition is in English. Quotations stay in the original Serbian, in Latin script,
exactly as published. The question under each bet is the keeper's own wording and is the only
thing that is resolved. English glosses of Serbian words in the text are the keeper's; they were
made with the help of a language model and have not yet been checked by a native speaker.

The speaker in this book is the City of Belgrade as an institution. Names of officials are used
only to name a source. A bet is resolved "No" only with a primary source (a news item or page of
the City, a timetable of the city transport company GSP, a notice on the Serbian public
procurement portal, a decision of the City Assembly, a dated photo) and a date; if in doubt, it is
marked `strittig` (disputed). Pages of the City may be updated in place, so an entry can also be
resolved by a dated copy of the page.

Quotations remain (c) Grad Beograd. The website's terms of use (“Uslovi korišćenja”,
www.beograd.rs/lat/uslovi-koriscenja, read 20 October 2026) say that free information and
announcements of the City of Belgrade may be taken without restriction, naming the source with a
link to www.beograd.rs (“mogu se preuzimati bez ograničenja, uz obavezno navođenje izvora s linkom
na www.beograd.rs”); the City's official marks and content taken from other sources may not be
used. This book therefore quotes one sentence per bet, with the title of the news item, its date
and a link to www.beograd.rs, and no pictures, logos or coats of arms. Not checked by a lawyer.
This book is not endorsed by Grad Beograd; the City has not agreed with it or with any question in
it. Everything else CC0. The source pages are not copied into this repository. For each source
there is a link to a Wayback Machine copy and the SHA-256 checksum of the keeper's local copy.
Quotes were checked against the text of the page after removing markup, decoding entities and
collapsing repeated spaces.

The "Computer" in this book is a language model (Claude, Anthropic) with access to public
sources, not special software. It stands in the same list as the City, under the same rules.

Corrections: write to hallo@belegbar.eu (Serbian, English and German are fine). Complaints are
checked within 48 hours and noted on the bet concerned; entries are never changed, only
annotated.

The keeper takes no money from the institutions measured here, with one exception: a published
fixed price for entries an institution lodges itself (festgehalten format v1, §8.4). So far:
none.

Expiry: an open bet expires two years after its check date (format §1.1, §2.3). Expired bets do
not count towards the hit rate, but they stay in the record.
