Antwerp keeps saying when things will be done: the winners of its accessibility prize on
1 December, a permit application for the river banks on the Left Bank by the end of the year, a
waste container for every business from 2027, a new pontoon at the Steenplein and new terraces on
the Wapper by spring 2027, a new park on old railway ground by summer 2027, 100 hectares of extra
park by 2030. This book writes it down, waits, and checks. It quotes one speaker: the City of
Antwerp (Stad Antwerpen, with its College of Mayor and Aldermen), in the press releases on its own
press website. A statement goes in if it has a date; intentions without one stay out. Each bet
asks only whether the date in the quote was met, never whether anyone meant it.

Why Antwerp: the keeper's books now cover very large cities and capitals only. Antwerp, the
largest city of Flanders and home of one of Europe's largest ports, comes after books on Brussels,
Rotterdam and other large cities of Europe, most recently Kraków.

Sources: the press releases of the City (“Persberichten van stad Antwerpen”) at pers.antwerpen.be,
in Dutch. The news pages of www.antwerpen.be load their list by script and gave no text without a
browser, so they were not used. The press site's robots.txt blocks only its subscribe pages. Its
sitemap listed 2,004 releases; the sitemap is sorted by the date a page was last saved, not by the
date of the release, so the keeper counted only releases first published from 1 March 2026: 193
releases from 2 March to 19 October 2026, all read on 20 October 2026. In them the keeper looked
for sentences that name a future date together with a promise word (116 sentences), then read each
candidate in its release. Twenty-one sentences from twenty-one releases were checked live and in
the archive; twenty became bets. The date a statement was said is the date of publication of the
release.

Weaknesses, said openly: most of the bets are about works on streets, squares and parks, because
that is what the City's releases promise most often with a date. Three bets (antwerpen-2026-003,
-004 and -019) are only about the next step of a design. Some works are shared with other bodies
(De Lijn in -008, the Flemish waterways company in -014, a doctors' circle in -017); the bet is on
the City's own sentence. One sentence (-007) is by the mayor of the District of Antwerp, a district
of the City, quoted in the City's own release. One bet (-020) is a target for 2030, not a promise
of works.

Left out on purpose: closures of premises ordered by the Mayor (an act, not a promise); works of
other bodies, even when the City reports them (tram 9 and the premetro tunnel of De Lijn, studies
on covering the ring road by Lantis and the Flemish government, church organs of the church
councils); events, Christmas markets and campaigns; neighbourhood car parks that had already
opened in part; targets without a measurable size; and a second sentence on the works of
antwerpen-2026-009 (“Drakenhoflaan … terug een echte laan”), which is too soft to be checked.

Reading the dates: a fixed day counts to that day; a month to its last day; “eind dit jaar” or
“tegen het einde van 2026” (at the end of this year, by the end of 2026) to 31 December 2026;
“vanaf 2027” (from 2027) as in force on 1 January 2027; “begin 2027” (early 2027) as the first
quarter, to 31 March 2027; “tot april 2027” (until April 2027) in favour of the City as finished by
30 April 2027; “voorjaar” or “lente” (spring) to 31 May; “zomer” (summer) to 31 August; “najaar”
(autumn) to 30 November; “tegen 2030” (by 2030) to 31 December 2030. “Voor de start van de volgende
Sinksenfoor” (before the start of the next Sinksenfoor fair) is read as Saturday 15 May 2027 until
the City names the day (antwerpen-2026-010). Where a sentence says works start in a season, the
keeper reads the start, not the end, and the bet says so (-016). Each bet says so. A quoted
sentence with “verwacht” (expected), “voorzien” (foreseen), “als alles volgens plan(ning)
verloopt” (if all goes to plan) or “we willen” (we want) counts as said with a reservation
(`voraussichtlich`, 0.80); a plain statement counts as announced (`angekuendigt`, 1.00). Only the
quoted sentence decides.

Elections: the current term of the Mayor and the City Council runs until the next ordinary
municipal elections in Flanders, due in October 2030 (six-year term since the elections of
October 2024; the keeper did not read an official source for the date).

Language: this edition is in English. Quotations stay in the original Dutch, exactly as
published. The question under each bet is the keeper's own wording and is the only thing that is
resolved. English glosses of Dutch words in the text are the keeper's; they were made with the
help of a language model and have not yet been checked by a native speaker.

The speaker in this book is the Stad Antwerpen as an institution. Names of officials are used
only to name a source. A bet is resolved "No" only with a primary source (a press release or page
of the City, a decision of the City Council or the College, an entry in the Flemish permit portal,
a dated photo) and a date; if in doubt, it is marked `strittig` (disputed). Press releases can be
saved again later under the same address, so an entry can also be resolved by a dated copy of the
page.

Quotations remain (c) Stad Antwerpen. The keeper found no terms of use for the text of the press
releases on pers.antwerpen.be (read 20 October 2026). Many releases say that their pictures are
“uitsluitend bedoeld voor publicatie in de pers” (meant only for publication in the press); this
book uses no pictures, logos or coats of arms. It quotes one sentence per bet, with the title of
the release, its date and a link to pers.antwerpen.be. Short quotations rest on the right to quote
in Belgian law (Wetboek van economisch recht, art. XI.190, 1°); not checked by a lawyer. This book
is not endorsed by the Stad Antwerpen; the City has not agreed with it or with any question in it.
Everything else CC0. The source pages are not copied into this repository. For each source there
is a link to a Wayback Machine copy and the SHA-256 checksum of the keeper's local copy. Contact
details of press officers at the end of the releases are never used. Quotes were checked against
the text of the release after removing markup, decoding entities and collapsing repeated spaces.

The "Computer" in this book is a language model (Claude, Anthropic) with access to public
sources, not special software. It stands in the same list as the City, under the same rules.

Corrections: write to hallo@belegbar.eu (Dutch, English and German are fine). Complaints are
checked within 48 hours and noted on the bet concerned; entries are never changed, only
annotated.

The keeper takes no money from the institutions measured here, with one exception: a published
fixed price for entries an institution lodges itself (festgehalten format v1, §8.4). So far:
none.

Expiry: an open bet expires two years after its check date (format §1.1, §2.3). Expired bets do
not count towards the hit rate, but they stay in the record.
