Brussels keeps saying what will be built and when: trees in the old town, a new square, metro
stairs, parks, social housing, a restored conservatory. This book writes it down, waits, and
checks. It quotes two speakers. The City of Brussels (Ville de Bruxelles, the municipality, not
the Brussels-Capital Region) speaks on its own website and in the newsroom of its alderwoman for
urban planning. Beliris, the federal builder for Brussels (an agreement between the federal
state and the Region), announces its building sites on beliris.be. Each bet names its speaker.
A statement goes in if it has a date; intentions without one stay out. Each bet asks only
whether the date in the quote was met, never whether anyone meant it.

Why Brussels: the keeper's books now cover very large cities and capitals only. Brussels comes
after London, Paris, Hamburg, Munich, Vienna, Rome, Dublin, Copenhagen, Helsinki and Oslo.

Sources. City: the sitemap of www.bruxelles.be lists 4,538 pages; of the 1,842 changed since
July 2026, the 614 French pages and 54 older project pages were read on 18 October 2026. The
City's own press list only links out; building and planning news runs through the newsroom of
alderwoman Anaïs Maes (anais-maes.prezly.com), of which the 24 releases of 2025 and 2026 were
read. Beliris: the sitemap of www.beliris.be lists 2,142 pages; its French news items from
January 2026 and project pages changed since June 2025 (194 pages) were read on the same day.
In all these pages the keeper looked for sentences that name a date together with a building
verb, then read each candidate in its page. Eighteen became bets: six of the City, twelve of
Beliris. The date a statement was said is the date printed on the news item; where a page
carries no date, it is the last change of the page in the sitemap.

Left out on purpose: a street repair whose length comes with a weather reservation in the same
sentence (rue de l'Association); two lines of a table of building sites run by other bodies
(STIB metro station Gare Centrale, place Sainctelette); a study that “sera affinée” (will be
refined), which cannot be checked; a park whose works start “au plus tôt” (at the earliest) in
2028; dates already past; events, exhibitions and weekly track works of the railways. The
public transport company STIB closes its website to this kind of reader in its robots.txt; it
was not fetched. Where the City speaks about a project of another builder (the railway station
Bockstael, built by Infrabel and SNCB), the City stays the speaker of the quote.

Reading the dates: a month counts to its last day; “automne” (autumn) to 30 November,
“printemps” (spring) to 30 June, “été” (summer) to 31 August; a year alone (“en 2028”) and
“fin” of a year (end of the year) to 31 December. “Début” of a year (early in the year) has no
fixed end; the keeper sets 31 March and the bet says so. A duration (“environ dix-huit mois”,
about eighteen months, from May 2026) counts to the last day of the month it ends in. A quoted
sentence with “devrait” or “devraient” (should), “est prévu(e)” (is planned) or “environ”
(about) counts as said with a reservation (`voraussichtlich`, 0.80); a plain statement or
schedule line counts as announced (`angekuendigt`, 1.00). Only the quoted sentence decides;
a general note elsewhere on the page (for example that weather can change the planning) is
recorded in the bet, not counted.

Language: this edition is in English. Quotations stay in the original French, exactly as
published, including the sources' own typos. The question under each bet is the keeper's own
wording and is the only thing that is resolved. English glosses of French words in the text
are the keeper's.

Speakers in this book are the Ville de Bruxelles and Beliris as institutions. The newsroom of
the alderwoman is read as the City speaking; names of officials are used only to name a
source. A bet is resolved "No" only with a primary source (a page or news item of the City or
of Beliris, a decision of the City Council, a permit, a dated photo) and a date; if in doubt,
it is marked `strittig` (disputed). Pages of both speakers are updated in place, so an entry
can also be resolved by a dated copy of the page.

Quotations remain (c) Ville de Bruxelles and Beliris respectively (short quotation for review
and discussion under Belgian copyright law, Code de droit économique art. XI.189; re-use terms
of the two websites were not checked; not checked by a lawyer); everything else CC0. The
source pages are not copied into this repository. For each source there is a link to a Wayback

the text after removing inline markup and collapsing repeated spaces.

The "Computer" in this book is a language model (Claude, Anthropic) with access to public
sources, not special software. It stands in the same list as the City and Beliris, under the
same rules.

Corrections: write to hallo@belegbar.eu (French, Dutch, English and German are fine).
Complaints are checked within 48 hours and noted on the bet concerned; entries are never
changed, only annotated.

The keeper takes no money from the institutions measured here, with one exception: a
published fixed price for entries an institution lodges itself (festgehalten format v1, §8.4).
So far: none.

Expiry: an open bet expires two years after its check date (format §1.1, §2.3). Expired bets
do not count towards the hit rate, but they stay in the record.
