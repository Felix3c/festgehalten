Zaragoza keeps saying what it will do and when: 23 new electric buses and the first stretch of
the renewed river Huerva by the end of the year, the Centro Cívico Hispanidad and the Parque del
Tío Jorge at the beginning of 2027, the fountain in the plaza del Pilar by summer 2027, 600 more
public bicycles during 2027. This book writes it down, waits, and checks. It quotes one speaker:
the City of Zaragoza (Ayuntamiento de Zaragoza, with Mayor Natalia Chueca and the members of her
executive, the Gobierno de Zaragoza), in the news items of its press office on its own website. A
statement goes in if it has a date; intentions without one stay out. Each bet asks only whether
the date in the quote was met, never whether anyone meant it.

Why Zaragoza: the keeper's books now cover very large cities and capitals only. Zaragoza, the
fifth-largest city in Spain, comes after books on Naples, Palermo, Porto and other European
capitals and large cities.

Sources: the news items of the City's press office (Gabinete de Prensa) on www.zaragoza.es
(“Noticias”, in Spanish). The website's robots.txt (read 23 October 2026) excludes, among other
things, the search, the API and the CSV and JSON versions of its lists; this book used none of
them, only the public HTML list of news items. The keeper read 750 items back to 20 April 2026, on
23 October 2026; 716 of them are dated 20 April to 23 October 2026 (about four a day). Most are
events, exhibitions, deadlines for applications and traffic notices. In them the keeper looked for
sentences that name a future date together with a promise word (242 sentences in 162 items), then
read each candidate in its item. Seventeen sentences were checked live and in the archive; sixteen
became bets. Every item names as its author the Ayuntamiento de Zaragoza and its press office. The
date a statement was said is the creation date on the item's own page.

Left out on purpose: works and plans of others, even when the City reports them (companies
expanding factories, investors, the national tourism plan, the electricity grid, private
universities, EU programmes); events, exhibitions and deadlines with a closing day; titles without
a checkable act (European Capital of Sport 2027); targets without a checkable end (the climate aim
for 2030); a sentence whose year is probably a slip (works in the Barranco de la Muerte to end “in
the spring of this year”, written in October); and deadlines that had already passed on 23
October 2026. One promise was left out because it had already been kept before this book began:
the monthly flower and pet market in the plaza José María Forqué, promised “from this autumn”,
opened on 6 September 2026. One bet rests on a building that a company builds on City land under
a building right (the summer pools of the CDM La Aljafería); it is in because the City's own item
gives the date.

Reading the dates: “este otoño” (this autumn) means by 21 December 2026; “a finales de este año”,
“antes de que acabe el año” and “antes de final de año” (at the end of this year, before the year
ends) by 31 December 2026; “enero de 2027” (January 2027) by 31 January 2027; “principios de
2027” and “el primer trimestre del año 2027” (the beginning of 2027, the first quarter of 2027) by
31 March 2027; “el primer semestre de 2027” and “la primera parte de 2027” (the first half, the
first part of 2027) by 30 June 2027; “a lo largo del año 2027”, “de cara a 2027” and “en 2027”
(during 2027, for 2027, in 2027) by 31 December 2027. Each bet says so. Plain sentences
(“abrirá”, “comenzarán”, “finalizarán”, will open, will begin, will end) count as announced
(`angekuendigt`, 1.00). A sentence with “prevista”, “previsto”, “previsión”, “proyectada”, “el
objetivo” (foreseen, forecast, planned, the aim) counts as said with a reservation
(`voraussichtlich`, 0.80). Where one sentence holds two statements, the bet names the part it
rests on. Only the quoted sentence decides.

Language: this edition is in English. Quotations stay in the original Spanish, exactly as
published, including the City's own typing errors (“ne he marcado” for “me he marcado”). The
question under each bet is the keeper's own wording and is the only thing that is resolved.
English glosses of Spanish words in the text are the keeper's; they were made with the help of a
language model and have not yet been checked by a native speaker.

The speaker in this book is the City of Zaragoza as an institution. Names of officials are used
only to name a source. A bet is resolved "No" only with a primary source (a news item or page of
the City, a decision of the Gobierno de Zaragoza or the City Council, a procurement notice, a
dated photo) and a date; if in doubt, it is marked `strittig` (disputed). Pages of the City may be
updated in place, so an entry can also be resolved by a dated copy of the page.

Origen de los datos: Ayuntamiento de Zaragoza (www.zaragoza.es). The City's legal notice (“Aviso
legal”, read 23 October 2026) sets general conditions for reusing the City's documents and says
that they allow reuse “para fines comerciales y no comerciales” (for commercial and non-commercial
purposes), on condition that the meaning is not changed, the source is named, the date of the
last update is given where the document has one, and nothing suggests that the City supports the
reuse. This book quotes single sentences word for word, names the source with a link and the date
of each item, and uses no pictures or coats of arms. Short quotation for criticism and discussion
is also allowed by art. 32 of the Spanish copyright law (Ley de Propiedad Intelectual) (not checked
by a lawyer). This book is not endorsed by the City of Zaragoza; the City does not support it and
has not agreed with it or with any question in it. Everything else CC0. The source pages are not
copied into this repository. For each source there is a link to a Wayback Machine copy and the
SHA-256 checksum of the keeper's local copy. Quotes were checked against the text of the page
after removing inline markup and collapsing repeated spaces. The City's pages carry menu codes that
change on every visit, so a later copy of a page is never byte-for-byte the same; the quote is
what was checked.

The "Computer" in this book is a language model (Claude, Anthropic) with access to public
sources, not special software. It stands in the same list as the City, under the same rules.

Corrections: write to hallo@belegbar.eu (Spanish, English and German are fine). Complaints are
checked within 48 hours and noted on the bet concerned; entries are never changed, only
annotated.

The keeper takes no money from the institutions measured here, with one exception: a published
fixed price for entries an institution lodges itself (festgehalten format v1, §8.4). So far:
none.

Expiry: an open bet expires two years after its check date (format §1.1, §2.3). Expired bets do
not count towards the hit rate, but they stay in the record.
