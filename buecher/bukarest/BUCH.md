Bucharest keeps saying what it will do and when: 122 charging stations for electric buses by the
end of 2026, the Basarab overpass finally handed over early in 2027, all 25 district-heating
sections finished by December 2027, old cars out of the centre from 1 November 2027, the debt to
52,000 people with disabilities paid off by 1 November 2030. This book writes it down, waits, and
checks. It quotes one speaker: the City Hall of the Municipality of Bucharest (Primăria
Municipiului București, with the Mayor General and the General Council, Consiliul General), in the
press releases on its own website. A statement goes in if it has a date; intentions without one
stay out. Each bet asks only whether the date in the quote was met, never whether anyone meant it.

Why Bucharest: the keeper's books now cover very large cities and capitals only. Bucharest, the
capital of Romania, comes after books on other European capitals and large cities, most recently
Lyon, Rotterdam and Riga.

Sources: the press releases of www.pmb.ro (“Știri”, in Romanian). The website is a web app that
loads each text from the City's own open interface (api.pmb.ro), which allows all robots. The
keeper read the 345 press releases dated 19 April to 16 October 2026 through that interface on
19 October 2026. In them the keeper looked for sentences that name a future date together with a
promise word (84 sentences), then read each candidate in its item. Twenty sentences from 17 press
releases became 20 bets; three items give two bets each (bukarest-2026-001 and -017,
bukarest-2026-009 and -010, bukarest-2026-012 and -020). Each bet links the page of the press
release; its archive note gives the interface address the text comes from. The press releases
carry no author line; the sender is the City Hall. The date a statement was said is the release
date shown with the item.

Left out on purpose: works of other bodies, even when the City reports them (the national roads
company CNAIR, the metropolitan association ADIZMB with the Ministry of Transport, the six sector
city halls, the water concessionaire Apa Nova, Metrorex), and joint programmes with the
Government; deadlines that had already passed on 19 October 2026; events and seasons of the City's
theatres; wishes (“vrem”, “își dorește”, we want, he wishes) and plans without a checkable result.
One candidate, the statue of King Ferdinand I on Piața Victoriei by the end of 2026, is left out
because the Wayback Machine could not save its source on 19 October 2026 (three tries, error 520).

Reading the dates: a fixed day counts to that day; a month to its last day (“până în decembrie
2027”, by December 2027, is read in favour of the City as by 31 December); “la sfârșitul lunii”
(at the end of the month) to its last day; “până la finalul anului” (by the end of the year), “anul
viitor” (next year) and a year alone to 31 December; “la începutul anului viitor” (at the
beginning of next year) to the end of the first quarter, 31 March; “în primăvara” (in spring) to
31 May; “toamna” (autumn) and “până în iarnă” (before winter) to 30 November; “până în 2030”
(until 2030) is read in favour of the City as the end of that year. Each bet says so. Many
sentences of the City are plain (“va fi gata”, will be ready; “trebuie să fie gata”, must be
ready, used for deadlines in funding contracts); they count as announced (`angekuendigt`, 1.00).
A quoted sentence with “estimat”, “estimate” (estimated) or “putem” (we can) counts as said with a
reservation (`voraussichtlich`, 0.80). Only the quoted sentence decides.

Language: this edition is in English. Quotations stay in the original Romanian, exactly as
published. The question under each bet is the keeper's own wording and is the only thing that is
resolved. English glosses of Romanian words in the text are the keeper's; they were made with the
help of a language model and have not yet been checked by a native speaker.

The speaker in this book is the City of Bucharest as an institution. Names of officials are used
only to name a source. A bet is resolved "No" only with a primary source (a press release or page
of the City, a decision of the General Council, a procurement notice, a permit, a dated photo) and
a date; if in doubt, it is marked `strittig` (disputed). Pages of the City may be updated in
place, so an entry can also be resolved by a dated copy of the page.

Quotations remain (c) Primăria Municipiului București. The footer of www.pmb.ro (read 19 October
2026) says: “Primăria municipiului București © 2022. Toate drepturile rezervate.” (all rights
reserved); the keeper found no open licence for the texts. This book is not endorsed by the
Primăria Municipiului București; the City has not agreed with it or with any question in it. Each
bet quotes one sentence, with the title of the press release, its date and a link. This rests on
the Romanian Copyright Law (Legea nr. 8/1996 privind dreptul de autor): art. 9 leaves official
texts of an administrative nature (lit. b) and news and press information (lit. e) outside
copyright, and art. 35 (1) b) allows short quotations for analysis, comment or criticism to the
extent the purpose justifies, naming the source and the author (art. 35 (4)) (not checked by a
lawyer). Everything else CC0. The source pages are not copied into this repository. For each
source there is a link to a Wayback Machine copy of the interface response, and the SHA-256
checksum of the keeper's local copy. Quotes were checked against the title and text of the press
release after removing inline markup, decoding entities and collapsing repeated spaces.

The "Computer" in this book is a language model (Claude, Anthropic) with access to public
sources, not special software. It stands in the same list as the City, under the same rules.

Corrections: write to hallo@belegbar.eu (Romanian, English and German are fine). Complaints are
checked within 48 hours and noted on the bet concerned; entries are never changed, only
annotated.

The keeper takes no money from the institutions measured here, with one exception: a published
fixed price for entries an institution lodges itself (festgehalten format v1, §8.4). So far:
none.

Expiry: an open bet expires two years after its check date (format §1.1, §2.3). Expired bets do
not count towards the hit rate, but they stay in the record.
