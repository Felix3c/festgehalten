Brisbane keeps saying what it will do and when: a library reopening on Saturday 7 November, a
ferry terminal finished by mid-December, road and intersection upgrades complete by late 2026, a
draft plan for Fortitude Valley and a final vision for Kedron Brook released before the year is
out, and construction on the Walter Taylor Bridge towers in early 2027. This book writes it down,
waits, and checks. It quotes one speaker: Brisbane City Council, on the project pages of its own
website. A statement goes in if it has a date; intentions without one stay out. Each bet asks only
whether the date in the quote was met, never whether anyone meant it.

Why Brisbane: the keeper's books now cover very large cities and capitals only. Brisbane City
Council is the largest local council in Australia, in the capital of Queensland and the host city
of the 2032 Olympic Games. It is the keeper's first book outside Europe.

Sources: the Council's project pages under www.brisbane.qld.gov.au (“Council projects”, “Transport
projects”, “Waterway projects”). The robots.txt of www.brisbane.qld.gov.au (read 25 and 27 October
2026) allows all paths. The keeper took the list of pages from the Council's sitemap and read 159
project pages and 73 community service announcements (June to October 2026) on 25 October 2026.
The announcements are almost only road closures and planned burns and gave no bet. In the project
pages the keeper looked for sentences that name a future date, then read them in their page.
Seventeen sentences were checked live on 25 October 2026; fifteen became bets, and all fifteen were
checked again, word for word, on 27 October 2026. Project pages are updated in place and carry no
date of their own. The date a statement was said is the last change of its page in the Council's
sitemap (read 27 October 2026), converted to Brisbane time.

Left out on purpose: road closures and other notices with a fixed end; deadlines for surveys and
feedback; dates that had already passed on 25 October 2026; and two sentences that
promise no end (“proposed project completion” for Lambert Road in late 2027, works at Downfall
Creek that “continue through to early 2027”).

Reading the dates: “by November 2026” means by 30 November 2026; “mid-December 2026” by 20 December
2026; “late 2026”, “later in 2026” and “by December 2026” by 31 December 2026; “early 2027” by 30
April 2027, the end of the first third of the year; “mid-2027” by 31 July 2027. Each bet says so.
Plain sentences (“is set to reopen”, “will be released”, “to begin”, a dated line in the Council's
own timeline) count as announced (`angekuendigt`, 1.00). A sentence with “expected”, “anticipated”,
“on track”, “weather and site conditions permitting” or “subject to weather and site conditions”
counts as said with a reservation (`voraussichtlich`, 0.80). Where one sentence holds two
statements, the bet names the part it rests on. Only the quoted sentence decides.

Language: this edition is in English, and the quotes are the Council's own English, exactly as
published. The question under each bet is the keeper's own wording and is the only thing that is
resolved.

The speaker in this book is Brisbane City Council as an institution. Names of officials are not
used. A bet is resolved "No" only with a primary source (a page or news item of the Council, a
Council or committee decision, a notice, a procurement notice, a dated photo) and a date; if in
doubt, it is marked `strittig` (disputed). Pages of the Council may be updated in place, so an entry
can also be resolved by a dated copy of the page.

collapsing repeated spaces.

The "Computer" in this book is a language model (Claude, Anthropic) with access to public
sources, not special software. It stands in the same list as the Council, under the same rules.

Corrections: write to hallo@belegbar.eu (English and German are fine). Complaints are checked
within 48 hours and noted on the bet concerned; entries are never changed, only annotated.

The keeper takes no money from the institutions measured here, with one exception: a published
fixed price for entries an institution lodges itself (festgehalten format v1, §8.4). So far:
none.

Expiry: an open bet expires two years after its check date (format §1.1, §2.3). Expired bets do
not count towards the hit rate, but they stay in the record.
