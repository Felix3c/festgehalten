Genoa keeps saying what it will do and when: a new sea wall below the cliff of via Capolungo and
the first lot of works at a school by the end of October, four new speed cameras from 1 November,
a social taxi for the Lagaccio district in November, the tender for the Stadio Luigi Ferraris in
the autumn and its award by the end of March 2027. This book writes it down, waits, and checks. It
quotes one speaker: the City of Genoa (Comune di Genova, with Mayor Silvia Salis and the members of
her executive, the Giunta), in the press releases on its own website. A statement goes in if it has
a date; intentions without one stay out. Each bet asks only whether the date in the quote was met,
never whether anyone meant it.

Why Genoa: the keeper's books now cover very large cities and capitals only. Genoa, the
sixth-largest city in Italy, comes after books on Rome, Naples, Palermo and other European capitals
and large cities.

Sources: the press releases of the City on smart.comune.genova.it (“Comunicati stampa”, in
Italian). The robots.txt of smart.comune.genova.it and of www.comune.genova.it (read 24 October
2026) exclude only technical and search paths, not the press releases. The press room has no page
list, so the keeper took the list of releases from the website's sitemap and read 483 pages, about
460 releases dated 20 June to 23 October 2026, on 24 October 2026; the City publishes about three to
four a day. In them the keeper looked for sentences that name a future date (260 sentences), then
read about a hundred of them in their release. Sixteen sentences were checked live on 24 and 25
October 2026; fourteen became bets. The releases name the City as sender. The date a statement was
said is the date on the release's own page.

Left out on purpose: reports of City Council sessions; events and festivals with a closing day;
works and plans of other bodies, even when the City reports them (the Liguria Region, the port
authority, the motorway company, associations); aims for 2030 or 2050; orders and programmes that
simply run until a date; deadlines that had already passed on 24 October 2026; sentences that only
say what will happen if something else happens first; and two sentences that are hard to check
from outside (187 new staff in 2027, the end of a rat-control campaign). The two stadium bets rest
on a project proposed by a private company, Genova Stadium S.r.l.; they are in because the tender
and the award are the City's own steps.

Reading the dates: “entro l’ottobre 2026” and “entro il prossimo 31 ottobre” (by October 2026, by
31 October) mean by 31 October 2026; “dal 1° novembre” (from 1 November) means in operation on 1
November 2026; “a novembre 2026” (in November 2026) by 30 November 2026; “il prossimo autunno” and
“a fine autunno” (next autumn, at the end of autumn) by 20 December 2026, the last day of autumn;
“entro il mese di dicembre”, “entro dicembre 2026”, “entro fine 2026”, “entro la fine del 2026” and
“entro la fine dell’anno” (by December, by the end of 2026, by the end of the year) by 31 December
2026; “entro fine marzo 2027” (by the end of March 2027) by 31 March 2027; “entro il prossimo anno”
(within next year), said in 2026, by 31 December 2027. Each bet says so. Plain sentences (“entreranno
in funzione”, “procederà”, “sarà elaborato”, will come into operation, will proceed, will be drawn
up) count as announced (`angekuendigt`, 1.00). A sentence with “prevista”, “previsto”, “contiamo”,
“si conta di”, “l’obiettivo”, “vogliamo andare cauti” (foreseen, we count on, it is expected, the
aim, we want to be careful) counts as said with a reservation (`voraussichtlich`, 0.80). Where one
sentence holds two statements, the bet names the part it rests on. Only the quoted sentence decides.

Language: this edition is in English. Quotations stay in the original Italian, exactly as
published, including the typographic apostrophe (’) where the City used it. Two quotes are shorter
than the published passage (genova-2026-004 ends inside a sentence, genova-2026-011 begins after the
speaker's name); nothing is left out in the middle of a quote. The question under each bet is the
keeper's own wording and is the only thing that is resolved. English glosses of Italian words in
the text are the keeper's; they were made with the help of a language model and have not yet been
checked by a native speaker.

The speaker in this book is the City of Genoa as an institution. Names of officials are used only
to name a source. A bet is resolved "No" only with a primary source (a press release or page of the
City, a decision of the Giunta or the City Council, a notice on the City's notice board, a
procurement notice, a dated photo) and a date; if in doubt, it is marked `strittig` (disputed).
Pages of the City may be updated in place, so an entry can also be resolved by a dated copy of the
page.

Source: www.comune.genova.it and smart.comune.genova.it (CC BY 4.0). The press-release pages link
to the City's legal notes on www.comune.genova.it (“Note legali”, read 25 October 2026). They say
that, under the principle of open by default (art. 52 of the Italian Digital Administration Code,
D.Lgs. 82/2005) and unless stated otherwise, including content of third parties embedded in the
site, the data, documents and information published on the site are released under the Creative
Commons Attribution 4.0 licence (“rilasciati con licenza CC-BY 4.0”). Trademarks, logos and other
distinctive signs of the City are not covered; this book uses none. It names the source with a link
for every quote and uses no pictures or coats of arms. Short quotation for criticism and discussion
is also allowed by art. 70 of the Italian copyright law (Legge 633/1941) (not checked by a lawyer).
This book is not endorsed by the City of Genoa; the City has not agreed with it or with any question
in it. Everything else CC0. The source pages are not copied into this repository. For each source
the keeper keeps a local copy fetched on 25 October 2026 and gives the SHA-256 checksum of its text
(title and paragraphs), and a link to a Wayback Machine copy where the archive has one. On 25
October 2026 the archive had a copy for one source only (genova-2026-007); the others are marked
and will be added. Quotes were checked against the text after removing markup, decoding entities
and collapsing repeated spaces.

The "Computer" in this book is a language model (Claude, Anthropic) with access to public
sources, not special software. It stands in the same list as the City, under the same rules.

Corrections: write to hallo@belegbar.eu (Italian, English and German are fine). Complaints are
checked within 48 hours and noted on the bet concerned; entries are never changed, only
annotated.

The keeper takes no money from the institutions measured here, with one exception: a published
fixed price for entries an institution lodges itself (festgehalten format v1, §8.4). So far:
none.

Expiry: an open bet expires two years after its check date (format §1.1, §2.3). Expired bets do
not count towards the hit rate, but they stay in the record.
